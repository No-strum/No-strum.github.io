About

From remote sensing to ocean-wave dynamics.

I am currently a PhD student (2024-2028) in Fluid Mechanics at Imperial College London. My research connects physical understanding of the ocean surface with modern sensing and computer-vision methods.

Before my PhD, I studied Remote Sensing Information Engineering at Wuhan University (2020-2024), where I worked on multi-view stereo, image processing, lidar&point-cloud and multispectral geospatial data.

I am especially interested in research where algorithms do more than recognize patterns: they become measurement tools for extracting physically meaningful quantities from complex environmental data.

Current PhD Research

Breaking waves, whitecaps and ocean-surface kinematics.

A computer-vision and remote-sensing framework combining stereo observations, automated whitecap tracking and a BERT-based classifier fine-tuned on more than 10,000 annotated whitecaps.

01

Imperial College London · Hydrodynamics Laboratory · PhD Project

Automated Whitecap Detection, Tracking & Wave-Breaking Analysis

My current PhD work develops methods for identifying and tracking whitecaps, estimating their motion from ocean imagery, and classifying breaking events with a BERT-based model fine-tuned on more than 10,000 annotated whitecaps. The aim is to connect image-derived breaking statistics with wave kinematics and the directional distribution of energy dissipation.

01

Stereo ocean observations. Use stereo reconstructions to map imagery into world coordinates and recover sea-surface geometry.

02

Whitecap detection & tracking. Improve automated segmentation, background removal and event tracking across different sea states. Estimate breaking-front velocity and direction from image sequences.

03

Transformer-based classifier. Fine-tune a BERT-based model using more than 10,000 annotated whitecaps to classify breaking events and improve the reliability of downstream statistics.

04

Wave-breaking physics. Study directional breaking distributions and their connection to energy dissipation and ocean-wave dynamics.

Automated tracking of whitecap evolution on the sea surface.
